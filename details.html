1. Complete reward specification

Table I gives names, forms, and weights. Below is the definition of each of its 25 terms as implemented, in the same order and under the same names. Notation follows the paper: \(i \in \{l, r\}\) indexes arms or feet, \(q\) are joint positions, \(v_{xy}^P\) and \(\omega_z^P\) the body-frame planar and yaw velocity, \(c = (v_{xy,d}^P, \omega_{z,d}^{P})\) the locomotion command, \(\mu_t = \mu_\theta(o_t)\) the mean policy action, and \(\phi \in [-\pi, \pi]^2\) the per-leg gait phase. \(\mathbb{1}[\cdot]\) is the indicator function, and \((\cdot)_+\) is the positive part,

$$ (x)_+ \;\triangleq\; \max(x,\, 0) $$

so a term written \((x)_+^2\) is zero wherever \(x \le 0\) and grows quadratically only beyond that point — a one-sided penalty with no reward for staying inside the bound. It appears three times below: in the joint-position limit, where only excursions past the soft bound are charged; in the foot contact force, where only force above the threshold is charged; and in the pre-touchdown velocity, where only descent faster than the safe speed is charged.

Arm residual−0.05 \(\lVert a_{\mathrm{RL,arm},t}\rVert^2\) evaluated on the buffered residual, which the action term stores after clipping and scaling. It is therefore \(s^2\lVert\operatorname{clip}(a_{\mathrm{RL,arm}},\pm c_r)\rVert^2\) with \(s = 3\) — a factor of 9 relative to the raw policy output.
EEF body twist−0.05 \(\sum_i \lVert V^{E}_{RE,i}\rVert^2\) over both arms, read from the controller's ground-truth twist rather than the actor's copy. Falls back to a rigid-body computation when no controller is present.
Arm action rate−0.5 \(\lVert \Delta a_t\rVert^2\) over the 14 arm channels of the raw action.
Non-arm action rate−1.0 The same over the 15 non-arm channels. The action is partitioned arm / non-arm, not arm / leg / waist: the non-arm group is everything except the 14 arm joints, so it carries the 12 leg joints and the 3 waist joints.
Mean-action accel.−0.10 \(\lVert \Delta^2\mu_t\rVert^2 = \lVert \mu_t - 2\mu_{t-1} + \mu_{t-2}\rVert^2\). Registered as two terms of equal weight, one over the 14 arm channels and one over the 15 non-arm channels. \(\mu\) is the deterministic policy mean supplied by the algorithm, not the sampled action — differencing the sample would mostly penalize exploration noise.
Arm joint velocity−0.05 \(\sum_{j\in\text{arm}} \dot q_j^2\), measured joint velocity rather than commanded.

Joint-position limit−1.0 \(\sum_j \left[(q^{\text{lo}}_j - q_j)_+ + (q_j - q^{\text{hi}}_j)_+\right]\) — linear excess, not squared. The soft bounds are the hardware limits shrunk about their midpoint, \(q^{\text{lo/hi}} = m \mp 0.5\,\rho\,r\), with \(\rho = 0.95\).

1.4 Regularization — feet and survival

Term\(w_i\)Definition
Foot proximity−10 The indicator \(\mathbb{1}[d < 0.15\ \mathrm{m}]\), where \(d = \lvert\cos\psi\,\Delta y - \sin\psi\,\Delta x\rvert\) is the foot separation projected onto the base lateral axis. It measures crossing rather than absolute distance, and it is binary, not graded.
Foot orientation−5 \(\sum_i \lVert g^{\text{foot}}_{i,xy}\rVert\) — the norm, not its square, so the gradient does not vanish as the tilt shrinks.

1.5 Regularization — command-gated locomotion

Each is masked by the commanded behaviour, so the term is inert unless the command puts the robot in the regime it polices.

Term\(w_i\)MaskPenalized quantity
Heading leakage−10 \(\lVert c_{xy}\rVert > 0.05 \wedge \lvert\omega_z^{\text{cmd}}\rvert < 0.03\) \(\bar\omega_z^2 + 0.15\,\omega_z^2\)
Lateral leakage−2 \(\lVert c_{xy}\rVert > 0.03\) \(\bar v_\perp^2 + 0.15\,v_\perp^2\), with the signed \(v_\perp = \hat c_x v_y - \hat c_y v_x\)
Turn-in-place leakage−2.5 \(\lVert c_{xy}\rVert < 0.03 \wedge \lvert\omega_z^{\text{cmd}}\rvert > 0.1\) \(\lVert v_{xy}\rVert^2\)
Standing yaw rate−2.5 \(\lVert c_{xy}\rVert < 0.03 \wedge \lvert\omega_z^{\text{cmd}}\rvert < 0.03\) \(\omega_z^2\)

Gait-cycle averaging. \(\bar\cdot\) denotes the running mean \(\bar e_k\) over the current gait cycle, maintained by a command-manager term. It accumulates only while the gait phase advances, so standing contributes nothing; it flushes when the left-leg phase crosses zero upward; and during the first 5 samples of a new cycle it falls back to the previous cycle's mean. The \(0.15\times\) instantaneous companion preserves a dense per-step gradient while the mean targets net drift.

1.6 Impact handling

Term\(w_i\)Definition
Foot contact force−2 \(\sum_i \left(\dfrac{(F^{\text{peak}}_{z,i} - F_{\text{th}})_+}{F_{\text{th}}}\right)^2\), \(F_{\text{th}} = 400\ \mathrm{N}\). \(F^{\text{peak}}_z\) is the maximum over all physics substeps of the control interval, read from a contact-force history buffer rather than sampled at the control tick. The term raises an error if that buffer is shorter than the decimation, so a single-substep strike cannot be missed.
Pre-touchdown velocity−2 \(\sum_i \mathbb{1}[\text{active}_i]\left((-\dot z_i) - 0.15\right)_+^2\), where \(\text{active}_i\) requires all three of: airborne (\(F_z < 5\ \mathrm{N}\)), near ground (clearance \(< 0.05\ \mathrm{m}\)), and a non-trivial velocity command (\(> 0.03\ \mathrm{m/s}\)). Mid-swing descent is unpenalized.

Swing-foot reference. The reference \(z^{*}\) is a two-piece cubic Bézier over the normalized phase \(x = (\phi + \pi)/2\pi\) with \(b(x) = x^3 + 3x^2(1-x)\): rising \(0 \to h\) via \(b(2x)\) for \(x \le 0.5\), falling \(h \to 0\) via \(b(2x-1)\) after, with peak height \(h = 0.09\ \mathrm{m}\). It is flat at both ends, so the commanded vertical velocity is zero at liftoff and at touchdown.

2. Observation specification and pre-processing

2.1 The pipeline, in order

Each term is computed in physical units, then passed through four stages:

$$ o \;=\; \operatorname{clip}\Big(\; \alpha_{\text{term}} \cdot \big( f_{\text{term}}(\text{state}) + \eta \big) ,\; \pm 100 \Big), \qquad \eta \sim \mathcal{U}(-n_{\text{term}},\, +n_{\text{term}}) $$
  1. Noise, then scale — in that order. The noise magnitudes below are therefore in physical units, applied before the scale factor. dof_vel with noise 0.05 and scale 0.05 means ±0.05 rad/s of sensor noise, contributing ±0.0025 to the network input — not ±0.05.
  2. Uniform, not Gaussian. \(\eta \sim \mathcal{U}(-n, +n)\), resampled every step per element.
  3. Noise is applied to the actor group only. The critic group sets enable_noise = False, so it sees clean values even for terms whose config lists a noise level.
  4. Global clip at \(\pm 100\) after concatenation, then a running mean/variance normalizer inside the agent (obs_normalization).
  5. No history stacking. Both groups use history_length = 1; temporal information enters only through the two explicit previous-action terms and the gait phase.

3. Action scaling

The 29-channel action is partitioned arm / non-arm, as \(a_{\mathrm{RL},t} = [a_{\mathrm{RL,arm},t}^{\top},\, a_{\mathrm{RL,other},t}^{\top}]^{\top}\): 14 arm joints and 15 non-arm joints, the latter covering the 12 leg joints and the 3 waist joints together. There is no separate waist group — the waist is commanded directly by the policy alongside the legs, which is why Table I says "non-arm action rate" rather than "leg action rate".

Both the controller and the policy emit normalized joint-position actions. The low-level layer converts them with a per-DOF action scale \(\alpha\):

$$ q^{\text{target}} \;=\; q^{\text{default}} \;+\; \alpha \left( a_{\text{GAC}} + s\,\operatorname{clip}(a_{\text{RL,arm}}, \pm c) \right), \qquad \alpha = 0.25,\; s = 3.0,\; c = 1.0 $$

so the effective joint-space residual scale is \(s_r = s\,\alpha = 0.75\) and the correction is bounded by \(\pm 0.75\) rad per arm joint. Leg channels use the same \(\alpha = 0.25\) with no pre-scale. This decomposition matters when reading a configuration file: the pre-scale of 3.0 and the action scale of 0.25 live in different places, and the pre-scale alone overstates the policy's authority by 4×.

4. Curriculum details not in Table II

4.1 In-stage annealing

The arrows in Table II are linear ramps within stage D, not steps between stages, held constant outside the ramp window. Annealing inside the stage avoids a discontinuous reward-scale jump, which matters for an off-policy replay buffer still holding transitions scored under the old kernel.

\(\sigma_v\) is the linear kernel; the angular kernel holds at 0.25 throughout and does not anneal.

4.2 Early stopping in stages A–C

The \(\le\) on the first three iteration budgets is an early-stop criterion: mean episode length \(\ge 990\) of 1000 steps, sustained for patience consecutive evaluations after a minimum iteration count. Stage A: patience 1000, min 5k. Stage B: 2000, 5k. Stage C: 2000, 10k. Later stages run their full budget.

The reported run consumed 201,911 iterations against a nominal budget of 210,000, so early stopping recovered about 8k iterations across the three standing and slow-walking stages.

6. E2E-RL baseline — exactly what differs

E2E-RL is the same recipe with the nominal controller removed. Because the comparison is the paper's main architectural claim, the complete difference is listed here rather than summarized. The FastSAC configuration is byte-identical between the two — every field in the algorithm config matches, so the comparison is not confounded by optimizer tuning.

6.3 Observations

The baseline's actor and critic each lose two terms, both of which only exist because a controller does: controller_arm_joint_command (14) and ee_body_twist / ee_body_twist_full (12). Actor: 16 terms → 14. Everything else — scales, noise levels, clipping, normalization — is identical.

The baseline also runs a separate pipeline module, and does not receive the controller damping-ratio or angular-velocity-sigma flags, neither of which has meaning without a controller. Stage structure, iteration counts, trajectory datasets, and seeds are the same.

7. Why these values

Reward magnitudes

The weights fall into three bands, and the band matters more than the exact number. Tracking terms sit at 1–5 and are bounded in \([0, 1]\) by construction, so their weights are directly comparable: end-effector position at 4.0 and orientation at 3.0 dominate the locomotion terms at 2.0 and 1.5, which is the task priority stated explicitly. Shaping penalties sit at 0.05–2.5 and are unbounded above, so they are kept small enough that the exponential terms remain the dominant gradient in normal operation. Constraint-like penalties sit at 5–10 (foot proximity, foot orientation, heading leakage) or 200 (termination): these are meant to be prohibitive rather than traded off. Termination at −200 against an alive bonus of +10 makes a fall cost roughly 20 steps of survival, so early falling is never locally profitable.

The alive bonus is 10.0 rather than the 2.5 used elsewhere in the codebase specifically because FastSAC is off-policy: a dense constant signal keeps early value estimates well-scaled while the tracking terms are still near zero.

Kernel widths

Every tracking term is \(\exp(-e/\sigma)\), so \(\sigma\) sets the error at which the reward falls to \(1/e\) — it is a tolerance, not a gain. The curriculum tightens \(\sigma_P\) from 0.02 to 0.001 (\(\sqrt{0.001} \approx 3\) cm to \(\approx 3\) mm of characteristic error) only once the policy can already walk and reach, because a tight kernel early gives a reward that is flat at zero across the whole reachable set and therefore carries no gradient. The combined \(L_2^2 + L_1\) form exists for the same reason at the other end: once \(e_2\) is at the millimetre scale it contributes almost nothing to the exponent, while \(e_1\) still varies linearly and keeps the final approach learnable.

Masks and gait averaging

The command-gated penalties are masked rather than weighted because the behaviours they police are only errors under some commands: lateral velocity is a fault when walking straight and the intended behaviour when strafing. Gait-cycle averaging exists because the instantaneous quantities are non-zero by necessity — a walking biped yaws and sways within every stride — so penalizing them directly trades gait quality for command fidelity. Averaging over a completed cycle isolates net drift, and the 0.15× instantaneous companion is kept only to preserve a dense per-step gradient.

Observation scaling and noise

Scales bring each channel to roughly unit range before normalization: joint velocities and end-effector twists at 0.05, base angular velocity at 0.25, and everything already \(O(1)\) — commands, gravity, phases, pose errors — at 1.0. Noise levels are set by how well each signal is known on hardware rather than uniformly: 0.001 on projected gravity, 0.01 on joint positions and base angular velocity, 0.05 on joint velocities, which is the noisiest estimate on the robot and the one most likely to cause a sim-to-real gap. The saturated position channel exists because raw millimetre errors are \(10^{-3}\) next to \(O(1)\) inputs and would be lost in normalization; \(\tanh(100\,p)\) puts them on the same footing without letting large errors dominate.

Where these came from

Values reached by iteration on the reported task, not by systematic search. The bands and the structural choices above are the reasoning behind them, but no ablation isolates individual weights, and the numbers should be read as one working configuration rather than an optimum.

8. Still undocumented

  • Trajectory-dataset generation parameters — sampling interval, step interval, interpolation method, workspace-bound weight, smoothing ratio, and the stage-F mixing ratios. These are baked into the dataset files and cannot be recovered from the training code.
  • Domain randomization beyond initial joint scale, commanded height, and the torque-RFI limit: link mass, friction, and push magnitudes.
  • Number of evaluation episodes and duration per trajectory behind Table III, and whether the hardware numbers come from one policy or are aggregated across seeds.
