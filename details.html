1. Frames and notation

Three frame types matter: the world frame \(\{W\}\) with fixed orientation, the manipulation reference frame \(\{R\}\) in which the end-effector command and the controller are defined, and the end-effector frame \(\{E\}\). We additionally define a Cartesian frame \(\{C_F\}\) whose origin coincides with that of \(\{F\}\) while its orientation is aligned with \(\{W\}\).

$$ g_{FE} = \begin{bmatrix} R_{FE} & p_{FE} \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in SE(3) $$

\(E\) denotes both the left and right end-effectors generically, so \(g_{RE}\) compactly represents the pair \((g_{RE_l}, g_{RE_r})\). A twist is \(V^{F}_{RF} = [v^{F}_{RF};\, \omega^{F}_{RF}] \in \mathbb{R}^6\), and the body-frame Jacobian is defined through \(V^{F}_{RF} = J^{F}_{RF}\dot q\).

The command at time \(t\) is

$$ c_t = \left(v^{P}_{d,x},\; v^{P}_{d,y},\; \omega^{P}_{d,z},\; z^{P}_{d},\; g_{RD}\right) $$

— planar locomotion velocities, desired pelvis height, and the desired left and right end-effector poses expressed in \(\{R\}\). Note that the reference frame propagates to the desired world trajectory as \(g_{WD} = g_{WR}\,g_{RD}\): motion of \(\{R\}\) moves the world-frame target even when \(g_{RD}\) is fixed. This is precisely why a pelvis-frame representation transmits locomotion-induced roll, pitch, and vertical oscillation into the commanded end-effector motion, and why the choice of \(\{R\}\) is a design decision rather than a convention.

2. Relative Jacobian on a floating base

GAC needs the body-frame relative Jacobian \(J^{E}_{RE}\) and relative velocity \(V^{E}_{RE}\), which describe end-effector motion relative to a moving reference frame. Simulator and deployment kinematics libraries provide Jacobians and velocities of individual frames relative to the world, not relative quantities between frames. We therefore construct the relative motion in the world-aligned Cartesian representation first and then transform it into the end-effector body frame.

$$ V^{C_E}_{RE} = V^{C_E}_{WE} - V^{C_E}_{WR} $$ $$ V^{C_E}_{WR} = \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{C_E C_R}} V^{C_R}_{WR} = \begin{bmatrix} I & -(p_{WC_E} - p_{WC_R})^{\wedge} \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} V^{C_R}_{WR} $$

giving the relative Jacobian and its body-frame form:

$$ J^{C_E}_{RE} = J^{C_E}_{WE} - \mathrm{Ad}_{g_{C_E C_R}} J^{C_R}_{WR}, \qquad J^{E}_{RE} = \begin{bmatrix} R^{T}_{WE} & 0 \\ 0 & R^{T}_{WE} \end{bmatrix} J^{C_E}_{RE} $$

\(J^{E}_{RE}\) contains all joints contributing to end-effector motion relative to \(R\), torso joints included. The full Jacobian is used to evaluate \(V^{E}_{RE}\), but GAC is applied only through the arm-joint columns — the remaining joints are left entirely to the RL policy.

3. Geometric admittance control

GAC is used here as a geometric task-space tracking controller that generates joint position targets from \(SE(3)\) pose error without a measured external wrench, so the resulting command can be read as a differential IK step. The desired task-space dynamics are

$$ M\dot V + K_d V + f_G(g_{RE}, g_{RD}) = 0 $$

with the geometric elastic wrench

$$ f_G(g, g_D) = \begin{bmatrix} f_p \\ f_R \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} R^{T} R_D K_p R_D^{T}(p - p_D) \\ \left(K_p R_D^{T} R - R^{T} R_D K_R\right)^{\vee} \end{bmatrix} $$

This feedback is left-invariant: for any \(g_l \in SE(3)\), \(f_G(g_l g,\, g_l g_D) = f_G(g,\, g_D)\). A common change of reference coordinate frame therefore leaves the feedback law unchanged — which is what permits the same GAC law to be used across \(\{P\}\), \(\{H\}\), and \(\{H_0\}\) without re-derivation.

Discretizing and mapping to joint space with null-space regulation:

$$ V_{D,t} = V_t + \Delta t\, M^{-1}\left(-f_{G,t} - K_d V_t\right) $$ $$ \dot q_{D,t} = J^{\dagger}_{\lambda,t} V_{D,t} + \dot q_{\text{null},t}, \qquad q_{D,t} = q_t + \Delta t\, \dot q_{D,t} $$ $$ J^{\dagger}_{\lambda} = J^{\top}\!\left(JJ^{\top} + \lambda_{\text{null}} I\right)^{-1}, \qquad \dot q_{\text{null}} = \left(I - J^{\dagger}_{\lambda} J\right)\left(-k_{\text{null}}(q_t - q_{\text{rest}})\right) $$

The nominal GAC action is \(a_{\text{GAC},t} = q_{D,t}\).

4. Residual RL and the observation

The policy output is partitioned into arm and remaining joints, \(a_{\text{RL},t} = \pi_\theta(o_t) = [a^{\top}_{\text{RL,arm},t},\, a^{\top}_{\text{RL,other},t}]^{\top}\). The applied arm action is

$$ a_{\text{arm},t} = a_{\text{GAC},t} + s_r \cdot \mathrm{clip}\!\left(a_{\text{RL,arm},t},\, -c_r,\, c_r\right), \qquad s_r = 0.75,\; c_r = 1.0 $$

while \(a_{\text{other},t} = a_{\text{RL,other},t}\). A zero residual exactly recovers the nominal controller, and the learned correction is bounded by construction. Both the controller and the policy act in the joint-position target space of the low-level PD controller, so the correction composes directly with the structured feedback command rather than through a second control interface.

The actor observation is

$$ o_t = \left(c_t,\; q_t,\; \dot q_t,\; g^{\text{proj}}_t,\; \omega_t,\; a_{\text{GAC},t},\; g_{DE,t},\; p^{\text{sat}}_{DE,t},\; V^{E}_{RE,t},\; a_{\text{RL},t-1},\; a_{\text{RL},t-2},\; \phi_t \right) $$
  • \(a_{\text{GAC},t}\) is in the observation. The residual policy sees the structured command it is correcting.
  • Pose error \(g_{DE,t}\) is represented by its 3D translation and the 6D rotation representation.
  • Saturated position feature. \(p^{\text{sat}}_{DE,t} = \tanh(100 \cdot p_{DE,t})\), retaining sensitivity to small translational errors while limiting range — the feature that makes millimetre-scale tracking learnable at all.
  • Gait state \(\phi_t\) supplies the sinusoidal phase variables used for locomotion.
  • Two previous residual actions provide the short-term history the acceleration penalty needs.
  • Asymmetric actor-critic. The critic additionally receives privileged simulation quantities including pelvis linear velocity, which is never given to the actor during training or deployment.

Under the approximately second-order GAC closed-loop dynamics, \(V^{E}_{RE}\) and \(g_{DE}\) together form an approximately Markovian representation of the task-space tracking state, which is why exposing them — rather than only the resulting joint target — is well-motivated.

5. Manipulation reference frame selection

The heading frame retains pelvis position and yaw while removing roll and pitch:

$$ p_{WH} = p_{WP}, \qquad R_{WH} = R_z(\psi) $$

The ground-attached heading frame additionally removes vertical heave:

$$ p_{WH_0} = \left[p_{WP,x},\; p_{WP,y},\; 0\right]^{\top}, \qquad R_{WH_0} = R_z(\psi) $$

\(\{H_0\}\) retains the planar \((x, y, \psi)\) motion that locomotion needs while removing the kinematic transmission of pelvis roll, pitch, and heave through the reference frame. Physical disturbances transmitted through whole-body dynamics remain, and are compensated by the controller.

The frame Jacobians are constructed from the pelvis Jacobian. With \(f = R_{WP}e_x\) and \(\psi = \tan^{-1}(f_y, f_x)\), the heading rate is

$$ \dot\psi = \left[-\tfrac{f_z f_x}{f_x^2 + f_y^2},\; -\tfrac{f_y f_z}{f_x^2 + f_y^2},\; 1\right]\omega^{C_P}_{WP} \;\triangleq\; a^{\top}\omega^{C_P}_{WP}, \qquad J_\psi = a^{\top} J^{C_P}_{\omega,WP} $$
$$ J^{C_R}_{WH} = \begin{bmatrix} J^{C_P}_{v,WP} \\ e_z J_\psi \end{bmatrix}, \qquad J^{C_R}_{WH_0} = \begin{bmatrix} P_{xy} J^{C_P}_{v,WP} \\ e_z J_\psi \end{bmatrix}, \qquad P_{xy} = \mathrm{diag}(1,1,0) $$

Since pelvis angular velocity is directly available from the IMU while linear velocity is not reliably measured at deployment, only the measured angular-velocity components of the floating base are used in the reference-frame velocity provided to the actor.

6. Reward specification

The reward decomposes as \(r_t = r^{\text{manip}}_t + r^{\text{loco}}_t + r^{\text{reg}}_t = \sum_i w_i r_{i,t}\). Final-stage weights are in the Table I panel on the project page; the definitions follow.

6.1 End-effector position: combined \(L_2^2 + L_1\)

$$ r_{\text{pos},t} = \exp\left(-\frac{e_{2,t}}{\sigma_2} - \frac{e_{1,t}}{\sqrt{3}\,\sigma_1}\right) $$ $$ e_{2,t} = \mathrm{mean}_{i \in \{l,r\}} \lVert p_{DE,i,t}\rVert_2^2, \qquad e_{1,t} = \mathrm{mean}_{i \in \{l,r\}} \lVert p_{DE,i,t}\rVert_1 $$

The squared term dominates while the error is large; the \(L_1\) term keeps a usable gradient once it is small, which is what drives the final approach to millimetre accuracy. Final stage: \(\sigma_2 = 0.001\), \(\sigma_1 = 0.01\).

6.2 End-effector orientation: intrinsic \(SO(3)\) trace error

$$ r_{\text{rot},t} = \exp\left(-\frac{\mathrm{mean}_{i \in \{l,r\}}\left(3 - \mathrm{tr}(R_{DE,i,t})\right)}{\sigma_R}\right) $$

6.3 Residual and smoothness regularization

$$ r_{\text{res},t} = \lVert a_{\text{RL,arm},t}\rVert_2^2 $$ $$ r_{\Delta a,t} = \lVert a_t - a_{t-1}\rVert_2^2, \qquad r_{\Delta^2\mu,t} = \lVert \mu_t - 2\mu_{t-1} + \mu_{t-2}\rVert_2^2 $$

The residual penalty keeps the learned correction a bounded correction rather than a replacement. The acceleration penalty acts on the policy mean action \(\mu_t = \mu_\theta(o_t)\), not the sampled action — penalizing the sampled action's second difference would mostly penalize exploration noise.

6.4 Locomotion and gait

A periodic gait state \(\phi_t\) follows phase-based bipedal locomotion, with left and right leg phases offset by \(\pi\). The desired swing-foot height is generated from \(\phi_t\) using a symmetric cubic-Bézier trajectory with a peak height of 0.09 m; its tracking error enters \(r^{\text{loco}}_t\).

6.5 Gait-cycle averaging for leakage penalties

For a signal \(e_t\) over a complete gait cycle \(\mathcal{G}_k\):

$$ \bar e_k = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_k|}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{G}_k} e_t $$

The gait-averaged yaw rate and command-perpendicular velocity are penalized for heading and lateral leakage respectively. Averaging over the cycle avoids penalizing the periodic motion intrinsic to locomotion — an instantaneous penalty on lateral velocity punishes the side-to-side weight shift that walking requires, whereas the cycle mean isolates net drift.

No retargeted whole-body human-motion data is used. For stages B, D, and E, diverse arm configurations are sampled and mapped through forward kinematics to feasible end-effector poses, then interpolated in \(SE(3)\) with randomized motion speeds to generate full-pose tracking trajectories.

8. Symmetry augmentation and regularization

Let \(\mathcal{M}\) denote the full reflection operator associated with the Cartesian reflection \(M = \mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 1)\), which exchanges left and right joints and applies the corresponding sign changes to directional quantities. For each replay transition, a mirrored counterpart is constructed by applying \(\mathcal{M}\) consistently to all reflection-dependent quantities: observations, actions, locomotion commands, end-effector targets, and gait-phase variables.

Beyond replay augmentation, the actor is regularized to be equivariant and the critic invariant:

$$ \mathcal{L}^{\text{actor}}_{\text{sym}} = \left\lVert \mu_\theta(\mathcal{M} \circ o_t) - \mathcal{M} \circ \mu_\theta(o_t) \right\rVert_2^2 $$ $$ \mathcal{L}^{\text{critic}}_{\text{sym}} = \left\lVert Q_\theta(o_t, a_t) - Q_\theta(\mathcal{M} \circ o_t,\; \mathcal{M} \circ a_t) \right\rVert_2^2 $$

Augmentation alone leaves the network free to satisfy symmetry only on the states it happens to visit; the explicit losses make it a property of the function. Both are added to the corresponding RL objectives.

For the static-arm-holding and world-frame tracking experiments, a global wrapper generates locomotion commands from the estimated planar pelvis pose \((x, y, \psi) \in SE(2)\) via a PD-like feedback law converting planar pose error into linear and angular velocity commands. The wrapper determines only the locomotion command; end-effector tracking and whole-body coordination remain handled by ManTra.

10. Baselines and evaluation protocol

BaselineWhat it changes
E2E-RLSame recipe as ManTra, nominal GAC controller removed. Isolates the value of explicit task-space feedback.
GAC+Split-RLArms controlled by GAC alone; RL controls only the remaining joints. Isolates the value of the arm residual.
SONIC-IK-WBTUpper-body joint targets from inverse kinematics, tracked by the SONIC whole-body tracking model.
SONIC-TeleopThe original three-point SONIC teleoperation policy, head and bilateral wrist targets.
SONIC-Teleop-v1.1Updated three-point SONIC teleoperation policy using a heading-frame representation.

All four benchmark trajectories are held out from training: the curriculum covers the corresponding task-space ranges and motion classes but contains none of the evaluation trajectories. They are evaluated while the humanoid maintains a standing posture, isolating manipulator tracking precision from locomotion-induced disturbance.

For fairness, all controllers are evaluated in the pelvis frame where supported. SONIC-Teleop-v1.1 is the exception — its released policy uses a heading-frame representation, so its errors are reported in its own reference frame. Errors are mean ± standard deviation over temporal samples and both arms; for the nonsmooth trajectory only steady-state samples after each command transition are included.

11. Still to be documented

Items not fixed by the paper, listed here because this page — not the paper — is where they belong. REPLACE each before publishing.

  • FastSAC hyperparameters: learning rates, batch size, replay capacity, warmup steps, number of critics, discount, target-update rate.
  • Actor and critic network widths and depths; the exact privileged observation list for the critic.
  • Symmetry loss coefficients for the actor and critic terms.
  • Trajectory-dataset generation parameters: sampling interval, step interval, interpolation method, workspace-bound weight, smoothing ratio, and the mixing ratios for stage F. These are baked into the dataset files and not recoverable from the training code.
  • Domain randomization ranges beyond the initial joint scale: link mass, friction, and torque-level randomization.
  • Number of evaluation episodes and total duration per trajectory in Table III.
  • Seeds, and whether the reported hardware numbers come from one policy or are aggregated.
