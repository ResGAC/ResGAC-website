2. Residual formulation

The policy output is split. Leg targets are commanded directly; arm targets are a bounded residual on top of the controller's nominal joint command \(a_{\text{ctrl}}\):

$$ a_{\text{arm}} \;=\; a_{\text{ctrl}} \;+\; 3 \cdot \operatorname{clip}(r, -1, +1) $$

The clip bounds the correction to a fixed joint-space envelope around the controller, so the policy cannot silently discard the controller. The residual magnitude is additionally penalized (penalty_arm_residual_l2), with the penalty weight annealed from \(-0.1\) to \(-0.05\) over the first half of the walking phase — strong early, so the policy first learns to walk without fighting the arms, then relaxed so it can correct the controller once locomotion is stable.

3. Geometric admittance controller

Per arm and per control step, the controller forms a geometric SE(3) wrench from the body-frame pose error, integrates a task-space admittance law, and maps the resulting twist to joint velocities.

$$ V_{k+1} \;=\; V_k \;+\; \Delta t \, M^{-1}\!\left(-f_g - K_d V_k\right) $$

with \(f_g = [f_p;\, f_R]\) the geometric wrench and \(M, K_d\) the admittance mass and damping. The system is run over-damped at \(\zeta = 2\). The task twist is mapped to joint velocities through a damped-least-squares relative Jacobian with a null-space rest-posture projector, then integrated to a joint target.

4. Reference frame selection

End-effector targets can be expressed in the pelvis frame, a heading frame, or a ground-heading frame — a yaw-only frame whose origin is the pelvis position projected onto the ground plane. The ground-heading frame is used throughout: it removes pelvis pitch, roll, and height from the manipulation target, so a target that is stationary in the world stays stationary in the target frame while the base bobs through the gait cycle.

6. Reward specification

Tracking terms use exponential kernels; penalties are quadratic or masked-quadratic. The weight table is on the project page; the definitions follow.

6.1 End-effector position, with a touchdown gate

Phases A–DE use a single Gaussian on the two-arm mean squared position error:

$$ r \;=\; \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert e_{\text{pos}}\rVert^2}{\sigma_{\text{eff}}}\right), \qquad \lVert e_{\text{pos}}\rVert^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\{L,R\}} \lVert p_i - p_i^{*}\rVert^2 $$

Phases F and teleop switch to a combined squared + L1 kernel, which keeps a usable gradient once the squared error is already small:

$$ r \;=\; \exp\!\left(-\left[\frac{\lVert e\rVert^2}{\sigma_{\text{sq}}} + \frac{\lVert e\rVert_1}{\sqrt{3}\,\sigma_{\ell_1}}\right]\right), \qquad \sigma_{\text{sq}} = 0.001,\; \sigma_{\ell_1} = 0.01 $$

The kernel width is modulated by an exogenous gait-phase gate, so that positional tolerance changes with the gait cycle:

$$ \sigma_{\text{eff}} = \sigma\left(1 + (s - 1)\,g_{\text{td}}\right), \qquad g_{\text{td}} = \max_i \exp\!\left(-\frac{1 - \cos(\phi_i - \pi)}{w^2}\right),\; w = 0.20\ \text{rad} $$

\(g_{\text{td}} \approx 1\) at touchdown (\(\phi = \pm\pi\)) and \(\approx 0\) at mid-swing. With the scale \(s = 0.25\), the width shrinks 4× at footstrike: the reward demands tighter end-effector tracking exactly at the moment the impact would otherwise disturb it. Because \(\phi\) comes from the gait clock rather than from measured contact, the gate cannot be gamed by altering the contact schedule.

6.2 End-effector orientation

$$ r = \exp\!\left(-\frac{e_{\text{rot}}}{\sigma}\right), \qquad e_{\text{rot}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i \left(3 - \operatorname{tr}(R_i^{T} R_i^{*})\right) \;\approx\; 2\theta^2 $$

No phase gating on orientation.

6.3 Locomotion tracking

$$ r_{\text{lin}} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert v_{xy} - v_{xy}^{\text{cmd}}\rVert^2}{\sigma}\right), \qquad r_{\text{ang}} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\omega_z - \omega_z^{\text{cmd}})^2}{\sigma}\right) $$

6.4 Swing-height tracking

$$ r = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(z_L - z_L^{*})^2 + (z_R - z_R^{*})^2}{\sigma}\right), \qquad \sigma = 0.008 $$

\(z^{*}\) is a cubic-Bezier reference profile over the gait cycle: stance → peak clearance 0.09 m at mid-swing → touchdown.

6.5 Impact shaping

Two terms shape landings, one on force and one on approach velocity:

$$ \text{contact force:}\quad \sum_i \left(\frac{\max(0,\; F_{z,i}^{\text{peak}} - F_{\text{th}})}{F_{\text{th}}}\right)^{2}, \qquad F_{\text{th}} = 400\ \text{N} $$

\(F_z^{\text{peak}}\) is the maximum over all physics substeps within the control interval, not the value at the control tick — a hard strike lasting one substep is otherwise invisible to the reward.

$$ \text{pre-touchdown:}\quad \sum_i \mathbb{1}[\text{active}_i]\,\max(0,\; -\dot z_i - v_{\text{safe}})^{2}, \qquad v_{\text{safe}} = 0.15\ \text{m/s} $$

\(\text{active}_i\) requires the foot to be airborne (\(F_z < 5\) N), near the ground (\(h < 0.05\) m), and the velocity command non-trivial (\(> 0.03\) m/s). Only the final descent is penalized; mid-swing velocity is free.

6.6 Gait-averaged drift penalties

Instantaneous yaw and lateral-velocity penalties punish the oscillation intrinsic to bipedal gait. Instead, a running mean over the current gait cycle is penalized, and the instantaneous counterparts are zeroed:

$$ \text{yaw leakage:}\quad m_{\text{straight}}\left(\bar\omega_z^{2} + 0.15\,\omega_z^{2}\right), \qquad m_{\text{straight}} = \left(\lVert c_{xy}\rVert > 0.05\right) \wedge \left(|\omega_z^{\text{cmd}}| < 0.03\right) $$
$$ \text{lateral drift:}\quad m_{\text{moving}}\left(\bar v_\perp^{2} + 0.15\,v_\perp^{2}\right), \qquad v_\perp = c_x v_y - c_y v_x $$

\(c\) is the unit command direction, so \(v_\perp\) is signed cross-track velocity. The \(0.15\times\) instantaneous companion retains a dense gradient while the running mean targets net per-stride drift. The mean accumulates only while the gait phase is advancing (standing does not accumulate) and is flushed when the left-leg phase crosses zero upward; during the first 5 samples of a cycle the previous cycle's mean is used.

6.7 Masked standing and turn-in-place penalties

$$ \text{standing:}\quad \mathbb{1}\!\left[\lVert c_{xy}\rVert < 0.03 \wedge |\omega_z^{\text{cmd}}| < 0.03\right]\omega_z^{2} $$ $$ \text{turn-in-place:}\quad \mathbb{1}\!\left[\lVert c_{xy}\rVert < 0.03 \wedge |\omega_z^{\text{cmd}}| > 0.1\right]\lVert v_{xy}\rVert^{2} $$

6.8 Posture and limits

  • penalty_pose_deviation: \(\sum_j w_j (q_j - q_j^{\text{def}})^2\), arm DOFs excluded. Waist weighted ~50, hip-yaw ~0.01, so the waist is held near default while hip yaw stays free.
  • penalty_dof_pos_limits: soft hinge activating at 95% of each joint's range.
  • penalty_close_feet_xy: indicator on yaw-aligned lateral foot separation below 0.15 m.
  • penalty_feet_ori: \(\sum_i \lVert g^{\text{foot}}_{i,xy}\rVert\), linear (not squared), penalizing foot roll and tilt.

8. Symmetry prior

Left/right symmetry is a sagittal-plane reflection \(M = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, 1)\), with \(M^2 = I\) and \(\det M = -1\), valid in the base, heading, and ground-heading frames because the lateral axis is \(y\). It is imposed twice:

  • Replay-batch augmentation. Each sampled batch is mirrored (joint permutation plus per-joint sign flips, with observations and actions transformed consistently) and appended.
  • Explicit losses. Actor equivariance \(\pi(Mo) \approx M\pi(o)\) and critic invariance \(Q(Mo, Ma) \approx Q(o, a)\), weighted 5.0 and 0.5 respectively.

The augmentation alone leaves the network free to satisfy symmetry only on-distribution; the loss term makes it a property of the function. REPLACE with the ablation result showing what each contributes.
